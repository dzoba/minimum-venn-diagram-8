\begin{abstract}
Bultena and Ruskey proved in 1998 that an $n$-Venn diagram has at least $L_n=\lceil(2^n-2)/(n-1)\rceil$ crossings
and conjectured that the bound is attained for every $n\ge 8$. We give an $8$-Venn diagram with $37=L_8$ crossings,
which settles the case $n=8$ and answers a question of Brenner, Gregor, M\"utze and Verciani; the best known before
had 39 crossings (Seamons). Its 37 crossings are listed in an appendix, and it has been checked by three separately
written programs (see the section on the use of AI tools) and in Lean~4. It has a half-turn symmetry, and in every
$8$-Venn diagram with 37 crossings a finite group of orientation-preserving symmetries has order at most 2.
Doubling gives a $9$-Venn diagram with 74 crossings, optimal among those in which some curve meets every other curve
exactly twice.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

An \emph{$n$-Venn diagram} is a collection of $n$ simple closed curves in the plane that intersect in finitely many
points and create exactly $2^n$ connected regions, one for each combination of being inside or outside each curve.
In any intersection point of at least two curves at least two of them must cross, and such a point is called a
\emph{crossing} \cite{BGMV}. Several curves may pass through one crossing, and some of them may only touch there.
Bultena and Ruskey \cite{BR98} proved that an $n$-Venn diagram, $n\ge2$, has at least
$L_n=\lceil(2^n-2)/(n-1)\rceil$ crossings, called diagrams that attain this bound \emph{minimum}, and conjectured
that minimum $n$-Venn diagrams exist for all $n\ge8$ (see also \cite{RW}). They are known for $n\le7$
\cite{BR98,BGMV}. For $n=8$, $L_8=37$. Brenner, Gregor, M\"utze and Verciani \cite{BGMV} constructed an $8$-Venn
diagram with 40 crossings and asked whether 37 is possible. Seamons \cite{Sea} reduced 40 to 39 by an edge surgery
on a reconstruction of their diagram.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
There is an $8$-Venn diagram with $37$ crossings. So the least number of crossings of an $8$-Venn diagram is $37$,
and the conjecture of Bultena and Ruskey holds for $n=8$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is a certificate, the dual graph of the diagram, given by its 37 faces in Appendix~\ref{app:faces}. The
certificate, three checkers and their logs, and a Lean proof are at \repo.

\section{The certificate}

We use dual graphs as in \cite[Section~1.3]{BGMV}. Let $Q_n$ be the graph on the subsets of $[n]=\{1,\dots,n\}$ in
which two sets are adjacent when they differ in one element, the \emph{direction} of the edge. The dual $Q(D)$ of an
$n$-Venn diagram $D$ has a vertex for each region, the set of curves containing it, and an edge across each arc of a
curve. It satisfies: (1) it is a spanning subgraph of $Q_n$; (2) it is a plane graph in which each face has length
$2\ell$ for some $2\le\ell\le n$ and contains exactly two edges of each of $\ell$ distinct directions; (3) for every
$j\in[n]$ the subgraphs induced by the vertices $x$ with $j\in x$ and with $j\notin x$ are connected. Conversely, the
dual of any subgraph of $Q_n$ with these properties is an $n$-Venn diagram \cite{BGMV}. The faces of $Q(D)$ are the
crossings of $D$. We work on the sphere; removing a point of a region gives a diagram in the plane.

Theorem~\ref{thm:main} rests on the converse, which \cite{BGMV} state without proof, so we give the argument. Let
$H$ be a connected plane graph satisfying (1)--(3), and let $H^*$ be its plane dual: a point in each face of $H$,
and for each edge $e$ an arc $e^*$ that crosses $e$ once and joins the points of the faces on its two sides. For
$j\in[n]$ let $S_j=\{x:j\in x\}$. The edges of direction $j$ form the cut $\delta(S_j)$, and by (3) both of its
sides induce connected subgraphs, so $\delta(S_j)$ is a bond (a minimal nonempty cut) of $H$. As $H$ is the plane
dual of $H^*$, the arcs $e^*$ with $e\in\delta(S_j)$ form a cycle of $H^*$ \cite[Proposition~4.6.1]{Die}; this
cycle, a simple closed curve, is curve $j$. The other edges of $H$ do not meet it and each edge of $\delta(S_j)$
crosses it once, so $S_j$ lies on one side of curve $j$ and the other vertices on the other side. Distinct curves
consist of distinct arcs, so they meet only in the points of the faces, finitely many. The union of the curves is
$H^*$, whose faces are the regions, one around each vertex $x$ of $H$. Taking the side of curve $j$ that contains
$S_j$ as its inside (the bounded side once a point of the region $\emptyset$ is removed), the region of $x$ lies
inside curve $j$ exactly when $j\in x$. By (1) there are $2^n$ regions, one for each combination.

A certificate lists the faces of a plane graph, each as the cyclic sequence of vertex labels around it, with
$x\subseteq[n]$ stored as $\sum_{i\in x}2^{i-1}$; so curve $i$ is bit $i-1$ in the files, the Lean code and the
legend of Figure~\ref{fig:diagram}. A face list in which every edge is traversed once in each direction, the faces
around every vertex form a single cycle, the graph is connected and $V-E+F=2$ is a combinatorial map of the sphere,
and it determines a plane graph with exactly these faces, unique up to homeomorphism \cite[Section~3.2]{MT}. The
certificate \texttt{venn8\_37\_halfturn\_a.json} (SHA-256 \texttt{7c1fed92\dots c41dd12}) has 256 vertices, 291 edges
and 37 faces, 32 of length 16 and five of length 14. So 32 crossings lie on all eight curves and five on seven.
Curves 1, 3, 5, 7 and 8 pass through all 37 crossings, curve 4 through 36, curves 2 and 6 through 35. At every
crossing some pair of curves crosses.

\section{The lower bound and the checks}

We recall why 37 is least \cite{BR98}. The dual of an $n$-Venn diagram is a connected plane graph with $V=2^n$
vertices, $E$ edges and $F$ faces, so $V-E+F=2$. Face lengths are at most $2n$ and sum to $2E$, so $E\le nF$ and
$2^n-2=E-F\le(n-1)F$, hence $F\ge L_n$. For $n=8$, $F\ge\lceil254/7\rceil=37$, and $F=37$ forces $E=291$ and
$\sum_f(8-\ell_f)=5$, where $2\ell_f$ is the length of face $f$.

The repository \repo{} contains the certificates, three checkers and their logs, the Lean project and the drawing
program. Theorem~\ref{thm:main} reduces to checking that 37 faces satisfy (1)--(3) and form a plane graph. Three
separately written checkers do this in a fraction of a second: \texttt{check\_a.py} checks (1)--(3), the induced
rotation system and Euler's formula; \texttt{check\_b.py} builds the primal diagram and checks that the arcs of each
curve form one closed curve; \texttt{ref37\_v2.py}, written from scratch by a separate Claude instance acting as
referee, also checks that some pair of curves crosses at each crossing. All three accept both 37-crossing
certificates, Seamons's 39 and his reconstruction of the 40 of \cite{BGMV}. They reject each of these with a face
removed, and two small inputs, built by the referee of this release, in each of which exactly one condition fails:
condition (3), or the vertex links (\texttt{python3 checkers/run\_all.py}).

For the face list \texttt{cert} of \texttt{venn8\_37\_halfturn\_a.json}, a Lean~4 proof (with Mathlib) in
\texttt{lean/} establishes
\begin{center}\footnotesize
\texttt{(IsVennDual8 cert}\ $\wedge$\ \texttt{CrossingAtEachPoint cert)}\ $\wedge$\ \texttt{cert.length = 37}\
$\wedge$\\ $\forall$\,\texttt{fs, IsVennDual8 fs}\ $\to$\ \texttt{37}\ $\le$\ \texttt{fs.length}.
\end{center}
\texttt{IsVennDual8} states (1)--(3) for an oriented face list and that the list is a combinatorial map of the
sphere (each edge traversed once in each direction, vertex links single cycles, connected, $V-E+F=2$);
\texttt{CrossingAtEachPoint} says that two directions interleave on every face. The facts about \texttt{cert} are
proved by kernel evaluation (\texttt{decide +kernel}), the bound by a general argument, using only the axioms
\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. The kernel evaluation, in
\texttt{CertProof.lean}, needs about 16~GiB of memory. Codex compiled the whole project from the same \texttt{.lean}
files under a different Lake configuration; the axiom list in \texttt{lean/AXIOMS.txt} is the output of that build,
whose log is not in the repository. With the Lake configuration as released we built \texttt{Defs}, \texttt{Cert},
\texttt{Decide}, \texttt{LowerBound} and \texttt{Sound}, every module except \texttt{CertProof} and the two that
depend on it, and confirmed that the bound \texttt{faces\_ge\_37} uses only the three axioms above. The
forward direction of the characterization of \cite{BGMV} and the passage from a combinatorial map to a drawing are
cited, not formalized.

\section{Symmetry and a second diagram}\label{sec:sym}

A symmetry of a diagram on the sphere is a homeomorphism mapping the set of curves onto itself. Every diagram has
infinitely many, for example any homeomorphism that is the identity outside a small disc in one region, so we count
them by their action on labels. A symmetry acts by $x\mapsto\pi(x)\mathbin{\triangle}c$ for a permutation $\pi$ of
$[n]$ and a set $c\subseteq[n]$, and this label map sends the face list onto itself, preserving the orientation of
every face or reversing all of them. We call such label maps \emph{automorphisms} of the diagram. Conversely, a group
$A$ of automorphisms is realized by a finite group of homeomorphisms isomorphic to $A$, acting on the drawing that
the face list determines, which is homeomorphic to the diagram: each automorphism permutes the triangles of the
barycentric subdivision of the decomposition into crossings, arcs and regions, and we map each triangle affinely
onto its image. So Proposition~\ref{prop:rot} also shows that a diagram with 37 crossings has at most two
orientation-preserving automorphisms.

With $\sigma=(1\,7)(2\,6)(3\,5)$ and $c=[8]\setminus\{4\}$ (the integer 247), $g(x)=\sigma(x)\mathbin{\triangle}c$
maps every face of \texttt{venn8\_37\_halfturn\_a.json} to a face with the same orientation: a half-turn. It fixes no
region, one arc (dual to the edge between $\{5,6,7\}$ and $\{5,6,7,8\}$, on curve 8) and one crossing (face 21 in
Appendix~\ref{app:faces}), on all curves but curve 4. A search over all maps $x\mapsto\pi(x)\mathbin{\triangle}c$
finds no other automorphism of the face list, including orientation-reversing ones, so the automorphism group of
this diagram has order 2.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:rot}
Let $G$ be a finite group of orientation-preserving symmetries of an $8$-Venn diagram with $37$ crossings. Then
$|G|\le2$, and a symmetry of order $2$ fixes exactly one crossing, one arc and no region.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The 37 crossings, 291 arcs and 256 regions are the cells of a decomposition of the sphere. Let $g\in G$ have order
$k\ge2$. By Ker\'ekj\'art\'o's theorem \cite{CK} $g$ is conjugate to a rotation, whose two poles are the only fixed
points of each nontrivial power of $g$. A cell mapped to itself by such a power contains a fixed point of it (for an
arc since a periodic map of an interval has one, for a region by \cite{CK} on its one-point compactification), so
the cells without a pole lie in orbits of size $k$. If $v,e,f$ count the regions, arcs and crossings containing a
pole, then $v+e+f\le2$ and $256\equiv v$, $291\equiv e$, $37\equiv f\pmod k$. If $k\ge3$ then $e=0$: an arc containing
a pole would be mapped to itself, and as a periodic map of an interval has order at most 2, the nontrivial $g^2$
would fix the whole arc. So $k\mid291=3\cdot97$. For
$k\in\{97,291\}$, $256\bmod k\in\{62,256\}$ exceeds $v\le2$. For $k=3$, $v=1$; but the fixed labels, solutions of
$\pi(x)\mathbin{\triangle}c=x$, form a coset of a subspace of $\mathbb{F}_2^8$ of dimension the number of cycles of
$\pi$, so there are $0$ or at least $2$. Hence $k=2$, and parity gives $(v,e,f)=(0,1,1)$. So $G$ is an elementary
abelian $2$-group. Its orbits on the 37 crossings have size a power of 2, so it fixes a crossing, and it acts
faithfully by rotations on the cyclic sequence of regions around it, since no nontrivial element fixes a region.
So $G$ is cyclic and $|G|\le2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
We sketch why a finite group of symmetries of an $8$-Venn diagram with 37 crossings has no orientation-reversing
element, so that it has order at most 2; this is not formalized, and for the two diagrams here the search above
settles it directly. Let $h$ reverse orientation. As $h^2$ preserves it, $h$ has order 2 or 4 by
Proposition~\ref{prop:rot}. Let $h$ be an involution. It permutes the 37 crossings, an odd number, so it fixes one,
and by \cite{CK} it is conjugate to a reflection, with a circle $M$ of fixed points (the antipodal map fixes no
point). Its permutation $\pi$ of the curves is an involution and fixes an even number $s$ of curves. If $h$ keeps
the two sides of a fixed curve $D$, it acts on $D$ as a reflection of a circle, so $D$ meets $M$ in exactly two
points. A fixed curve with its sides exchanged would be $M$ itself; then $s\ge2$, and another fixed curve would meet
it only twice, whereas any two curves share at least 32 crossings, since by $\sum_f(8-\ell_f)=5$ at most five
crossings miss a curve. So no arc lies in $M$, every invariant arc is reversed, and as $h$ has degree $-1$ its
Lefschetz number, computed from the cells \cite[Section~22]{Mu}, gives $f+a-v=0$ for the numbers of invariant
crossings, arcs and regions. Here $f$ is odd; $a\le2s$, since an invariant arc lies on a fixed curve and contains one of its two points
on $M$; $v\in\{0,2^{(8+s)/2}\}$, the number of solutions of $\pi(x)\mathbin{\triangle}c=x$; and $f\le7$ if $s>0$,
since the 32 crossings on all eight curves lie on a fixed curve and at most two of them on $M$. No case fits: for
$s=0$, $a=0$ and $f=v$ is even, and for $s>0$, $f=v-a$ is at most 0 or at least $32-16$. If $h$ had order 4, then
$h^2$ would be the half-turn of Proposition~\ref{prop:rot}, $h$ would map its unique invariant arc onto itself, and
$h^2$, the square of a homeomorphism of that arc, would preserve its ends and, being periodic, fix it pointwise;
but a half-turn has only two fixed points.
\end{remark}

The certificate \texttt{venn8\_37\_halfturn\_b.json} is a second diagram with 37 crossings, with the same face
lengths and symmetry $g$ and, by the same search, no other automorphism. It is not isomorphic to the first, even allowing relabelling of curves, exchange of inside
and outside, and reflection: the numbers of crossings on the curves are $34,36,36,37,37,37,37,37$ against
$35,35,36,37,37,37,37,37$, and the dual graphs have $196$, $50$, $10$ vertices of degree $2$, $3$, $4$ against
$192$, $58$, $6$.

\section{Nine and more curves}\label{sec:nine}

A face of the dual is \emph{colorful} if it has length $2n$ and contains complementary vertices $x$ and
$[n]\setminus x$. By \cite[Lemma~10]{BGMV} (Lemma~11 in arXiv:2511.09230v1, which contains its proof), a diagram whose
dual has a colorful face gives an $(n+1)$-Venn diagram with twice as many crossings and again a colorful face: two
copies of the dual, the second with $n+1$ added to every label, are joined by the edges $\{x,x\cup\{n+1\}\}$ and
$\{\bar x,\bar x\cup\{n+1\}\}$ for complementary $x,\bar x$ on that face. The face of
\texttt{venn8\_37\_halfturn\_a.json} containing $\{6,7,8\}$ and $\{1,\dots,5\}$ (face 1 in
Appendix~\ref{app:faces}) is colorful, which gives $n$-Venn diagrams with $37\cdot2^{n-8}$ crossings
(Table~\ref{tab:double}; for $n=16$ the 5118 of \cite{BGMV} is smaller). The files for $9\le n\le15$ were checked
by the three programs in \texttt{checkers/}; \texttt{checkers/invariants.py} confirms that each is the doubling of
the previous one.

In \texttt{venn9\_74.json} curve 9 meets every other curve in exactly two points. In this class 74 is optimal.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:class}
Let $n\ge3$, $m=n-1$, and let an $n$-Venn diagram with $F$ crossings have a curve $c$ that meets every other curve in
exactly two points. If $k$ crossings lie on $c$, then $F\ge2(L_m-1)+k\ge2L_m$; for $n=9$, $F\ge74$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Another curve $j$ meets $c$ in two points $p,q$, and has points on both sides of $c$, so one of its arcs between $p$
and $q$ lies inside $c$ and the other outside. Let $\Delta$ be the closed disc bounded by $c$ on one side, and
collapse $c$ to a point $P$. The other $m$ curves become simple closed curves through $P$, whose union is a connected
plane graph with vertices $P$ and the $I$ crossings inside $\Delta$, all of degree at most $2m$, and with the $2^m$
regions inside $\Delta$ as faces. Euler's formula gives $2^m-2=E-(I+1)\le(m-1)(I+1)$, so $I+1\ge L_m$. The same holds
on the other side, and $k\ge2$ crossings lie on $c$, so $F\ge2(L_m-1)+k$.
\end{proof}

This bounds one class only; in general only $L_9=64$ is known. Our search also found 74-crossing $9$-Venn diagrams
outside the class (\texttt{venn9\_74\_mixed\_r2.json}, \texttt{venn9\_74\_k3\_nearmiss.json}). Local searches from
these and other diagrams with 74 and 75 crossings found none with 73; they were not exhaustive and give no bound.

\paragraph{How the diagrams were found.} A Metropolis walk over plane spanning subgraphs of $Q_8$, given as rotation
systems, allowed unbalanced faces and disconnected curve halves at a cost and applied each move together with its
image under $g$. From half-turn invariant arrangements of the nested cycles of \cite{BGMV} it produced many diagrams
with 38 and 39 crossings, and walks from two 39s with a higher cost per edge reached the two 37s. The search programs
are not included.
